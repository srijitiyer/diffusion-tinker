# FLUX.1-dev amplifies RL noise SD3.5 absorbs

**The verdict: our FLUX+OCR arms are not a bug on our side, but they are also not a clean measurement of anything — they are the expected output of a configuration sitting on five documented cliff edges at once, two of which are intrinsic to FLUX.1-dev and three of which are ours.** The degenerate SDE window we found is real, but our own controlled experiment rules it out as the cause (roaming z=+0.94, no-window z=−0.06), and the literature explains why: the window is one of at least five fragility axes, and the two we cannot remove by configuration — FLUX.1-dev's bf16-only numerics and its guidance-distilled, CFG-free rollout — are precisely the ones the only published FLUX-vs-SD3.5 robustness comparison blames. V-GRPO states flatly that **"SD 3.5 M is inherently more robust to ELBO-based training"** while naive GRPO on FLUX.1-dev "suffers from severe training instability" ([V-GRPO, arXiv:2604.23380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23380)). Meanwhile **no published FLUX.1-dev + OCR RL result exists that worked** — the one serious attempt reports its FLUX hard-OCR improvements as "unresolved" with overlapping confidence intervals on a single seed ([λ-Controlled GRPO, arXiv:2609.22041v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22041v2)), and the only other FLUX+OCR datapoint in existence is a GitHub issue documenting over-optimization that could not be fixed by raising steps from 6 to 28 ([flow_grpo issue #204](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/204)). Our FLUX cell therefore measures run-to-run variance, not method effect; our SD3.5 cell, with its +0.188 gain and 0.008–0.041 between-seed spread, is the only one of the two that supports a method comparison at all.

## The verdict, stated plainly: setup-amplified, FLUX-inherent, and uninterpretable as published

Three claims, in decreasing order of confidence. First, **the FLUX instability is not attributable to a single broken knob on our side.** We found the degenerate window independently and tested it directly; neither treatment moved the mean (original 0.6194 ± 0.0576 with n=4, roaming 0.6538 ± 0.0281 with n=3, no-window 0.6169 ± 0.0364 with n=3). Whatever is driving a 0.093 terminal spread survives both fixes.

Second, **FLUX.1-dev is genuinely harder to RL than SD3.5-Medium, and this is published rather than conjectural.** V-GRPO's ablation is the key evidence: on FLUX.1-dev, removing either group-shared timestep-noise pairs or stratified timestep sampling "similarly destabilizes training," whereas on SD3.5-M "ablating individual components has a limited effect on both stability and performance" ([V-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23380)). The same paper reports that for FLUX.1-dev "the KL penalty alone is insufficient to stabilize training" — which maps exactly onto our own asymmetry, where the FlowGRPO-0.04 anchor cut the SD3.5 between-seed spread to **0.008** but left FLUX at **0.084**, essentially unchanged from the no-anchor 0.093. A regularizer that stabilizes one backbone and not the other is the signature of a backbone-specific pathology, not a config error.

Third, and most consequentially for how the result should be written up: **our FLUX configuration also sits well outside every published working recipe, in ways that independently predict high-variance advantage estimates.** We run group size G=8 where Flow-GRPO's own ablation reports that "when group size was reduced to G=12 and G=6, training became unstable and eventually collapsed, whereas G=24 remained stable throughout" ([Flow-GRPO, arXiv:2505.05470v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05470v5)) — G=8 is inside that documented collapse band. We run 4 prompts per epoch where every shipped FlowGRPO config uses **48 groups per epoch** ([config/grpo.py](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/blob/main/config/grpo.py)), and the repo author's advice on exactly this is unambiguous: "If you reduce the number of GPUs, please keep the hyperparameters unchanged. **Lowering the batch size or group size may cause the reward to stop increasing**" ([flow_grpo issue #172](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/172)). And the data budget asymmetry inside our own grid is severe: 4 prompts × G=8 × 80 epochs = **2,560 sampled trajectories** for FLUX against 8 × 8 × 150 = **9,600** for SD3.5, a 3.75× gap, against DanceGRPO's FLUX protocol of 32 prompts × 12 images × ~300 iterations ≈ 10⁵ trajectories ([DanceGRPO, arXiv:2505.07818v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07818v1)). The honest framing is that our FLUX arm is simultaneously the harder backbone *and* the thinner experiment.

So: not a bug, not purely inherent either. The FLUX cell is a real measurement of how variable RL on FLUX.1-dev is under a small-group, few-prompt, 6-step regime — and that is a different question from the one the grid is supposed to answer.

## Published FLUX.1-dev RL gains are small, narrow, and in several places negative

The asymmetry we observe mirrors the published asymmetry almost exactly. On SD3.5-Medium, Flow-GRPO reports **OCR accuracy 0.59 → 0.92** and **GenEval 0.63 → 0.95**, with the abstract claiming "very little reward hacking occurred" ([arXiv:2505.05470v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05470v5)). On FLUX.1-dev the same paper's entire result is Appendix C.4: a PickScore run evaluated on DrawBench, where PickScore moves 22.62 → 23.97, ImageReward 0.85 → 1.32, Aesthetic 5.71 → 6.02 — **and DeQA drops 4.31 → 4.24** ([arXiv:2505.05470v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05470v5)). There is no FLUX GenEval number and no FLUX OCR number in the paper, there is **no released FLUX checkpoint** (only three SD3.5-M ones), and the repo's FLUX GenEval evidence is a pair of W&B links with no tabulated numbers ([README.md](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/blob/main/README.md)).

DanceGRPO's FLUX table shows the trade more starkly. Optimizing HPS-v2.1 alone takes HPS 0.304 → 0.372 while **CLIP Score falls 0.405 → 0.376 and GenEval falls 0.659 → 0.561**; the dual-reward run recovers GenEval to 0.687 but surrenders roughly half the HPS gain ([arXiv:2505.07818v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07818v1)). SRPO's human evaluation then scores **DanceGRPO-on-FLUX at 5.3% realism against an untuned FLUX baseline of 8.2%** — a reward-optimized FLUX worse than its starting point on the attribute the reward was proxying ([SRPO, arXiv:2509.06942v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06942v1)).

Two structural observations compound this. First, **papers that headline SD3.5-M consistently relegate FLUX to a secondary, weaker or inconclusive position** — TempFlow-GRPO's FLUX claim is a 3.75× *speedup*, not a higher score ([arXiv:2508.04324v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04324v2)); λ-Controlled GRPO calls its FLUX results "directional support for cross-backbone generalization" with PickScore **tied** at −0.0001, 95% CI [−0.0013, +0.0011] ([arXiv:2609.22041v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22041v2)). No paper states in so many words that FLUX is harder; the pattern is in where authors put FLUX, with V-GRPO's robustness ablation as the one direct statement. Second, **the FLUX.1-dev baseline itself is reported inconsistently**: HPS-v2.1 appears as 0.304 (DanceGRPO), 0.313 (MixGRPO, [arXiv:2507.21802v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21802v2)), 0.313 (BranchGRPO, [arXiv:2509.06040v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06040v1)) and 0.289 (SRPO) — a 0.024 spread on the same model and metric, larger than several inter-method deltas those papers publish against each other. FLUX evaluation carries noise comparable to the effects being claimed.

Against this backdrop, our numbers are unremarkable in direction and remarkable only in that we measured the spread. **Our SD3.5 +0.188 sits credibly below Flow-GRPO's +0.33 and DDRL's +0.257**, which is what a G=8, 8-prompt, 150-epoch budget against their G=24, 48-prompt protocol should produce. Our FLUX pair of 0.5575 and 0.6507 against a 0.592 baseline is exactly the published FLUX pattern — small, bidirectional, metric-dependent — with the one thing nobody else did: two seeds.

## No one has ever published a FLUX.1-dev plus OCR RL result that worked

This is a finding, not a gap in our search. Across every source checked, the FLUX+OCR cell of the literature is empty or negative:

| Paper | FLUX.1-dev rewards used | OCR on FLUX? |
|---|---|---|
| Flow-GRPO | PickScore only (Appendix C.4) | No |
| DanceGRPO | HPS-v2.1, HPS+CLIP | No |
| MixGRPO / BranchGRPO | HPS-v2.1, PickScore, ImageReward | No |
| SRPO | HPSv2.1, Aesthetic, PickScore, ImageReward | No |
| TempFlow-GRPO | HPSv3 | No |
| GRPO-Guard | GenEval (both SD3.5-M and FLUX) | No |
| λ-Controlled GRPO | PickScore, hard-OCR, GenEval | **Yes — inconclusive, single-seed** |
| DDRL | none | **Never runs FLUX at all** |

λ-Controlled GRPO is the only published FLUX+OCR attempt, and it reports hard-OCR improvements as "unresolved" with overlapping confidence intervals, GenEval showing no significant difference across methods, and — critically — that "the broader **FLUX campaign runs single-seed** on 8 GPUs" ([arXiv:2609.22041v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22041v2)). The only other FLUX+OCR datapoint anywhere is a third-party reproduction thread: GRPO-Guard ported to FLUX-dev for text rendering, with "all parameters strictly per the paper and official code, unmodified," producing grid artifacts and severe quality degradation from optimization step 240 onward — **while both the reward and eval curves rose normally** — and explicitly noting that no such problem appeared when reproducing on SD3.5-M. Raising `num_steps` from 6 to 28 and adjusting noise levels failed to fix it ([flow_grpo issue #204](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/204)).

DDRL deserves its own sentence because it is the method our grid is testing. **The string "FLUX"/"Flux"/"flux" does not appear anywhere in any of the three arXiv versions of DDRL** (v1 3 Dec 2025, v2 6 Dec 2025, v3 24 Dec 2025, which a full text diff shows are substantively identical). Its only image experiment is SD3.5-Medium at 512×512 with an OCR edit-distance reward, described in a single appendix paragraph (§B.3), and the released code ships Cosmos-Predict2.5 configs only ([DDRL, arXiv:2512.04332v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04332v2); [cosmos-rl examples/ddrl.md](https://github.com/nvidia-cosmos/cosmos-rl/blob/main/examples/ddrl.md)). There is no FLUX cell in DDRL to compare ours against, and no cross-architecture robustness study of any kind in the paper.

**The practical consequence: we are the first people to attempt FLUX.1-dev + OCR RL with replication, and the field offers no prior against which to call 0.093 anomalous or normal.** That absence is the finding — and it also means a negative FLUX result from us is not a contradiction of anyone's published claim.

## The 1/σ² kernel, bf16-only numerics, and G=8 put our runs on five cliff edges simultaneously

The mechanism splits cleanly into what is measured, what is derived, and what is conjecture. Keeping those apart is the whole analytical content of this section.

**Measured and published.** The flow-GRPO Gaussian log-ratio carries an exact bias `E[log r_t(θ)] = −‖Δμ_θ‖²/(2σ_t²Δt)`, which GRPO-Guard derives analytically and then confirms empirically: the importance-ratio distribution's "mean consistently falls below 1" and its variance "varies significantly across timesteps," with "leftward mean shift and increasing variance at low-noise timesteps." **Gradient magnitudes vary ~20× across timesteps, dominated by low-noise steps**, so clipping "rarely triggers" at high noise while firing "excessively" at low noise ([GRPO-Guard, arXiv:2510.22319v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22319v1)). λ-Controlled GRPO names the controlling quantity — per-step "path variance," "determined exactly by the sampler's Gaussian transition kernel" — and reports that late low-σ steps are "precisely where the baseline systematically overshoots" its variance budget ([arXiv:2609.22041v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22041v2)). Since Flow-GRPO sets `σ_t = a·√(t/(1−t))`, σ_t → 0 at the clean-image end, so every one of these pathologies diverges at the late steps ([arXiv:2505.05470v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05470v5)).

**FLUX-specific and measured.** FLUX.1-dev is forced into bf16: the FlowGRPO README states that fp16 "provides higher precision than bf16, resulting in smaller log-probability errors between data collection and training," but "for Flux and Wan, becauase fp16 inference cannot produce valid images or videos, you will have to use bf16 for training" — and that log-prob errors "tend to be smaller at high-noise steps and larger at low-noise steps" ([README.md](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/blob/main/README.md)). Bf16 is a documented cause of hard failure on FLUX-family models: NaN log-probs traced directly to precision ([flow_grpo issue #177](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/177)), and GRPO-Guard's own author diagnosing FLUX over-optimization as "limited decimal precision of BF16 introduces rounding errors" causing importance-ratio deviation, fixed by moving training precision **from BF16 to FP32** ([flow_grpo issue #204](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/204)). V-GRPO's production practice encodes the same conclusion: bf16 for rollout, "full FP32 precision for master weights, ELBO-based surrogate computation for both π_θold and π_θ, and the backward pass" ([arXiv:2604.23380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23380)). This matters because **bf16 rounding error is a run-dependent stochastic perturbation that would present exactly as seed variance while actually being a numerics problem.**

**Measured, on step count.** Our 6 steps sit in a bracketed danger zone. Flow-GRPO found 10 training steps optimal and that reducing to 5 "does not consistently improve speed and sometimes slows training" ([arXiv:2505.05470v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05470v5)) — the turning point brackets 6. On FLUX specifically, V-GRPO needed **advantage soft-clipping introduced only when sampling steps were cut from 25 to 16**, where the 25-step setting had been stable with ratio clipping alone ([arXiv:2604.23380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23380)). Every published FLUX recipe uses 25 steps (DanceGRPO, MixGRPO, SRPO, BranchGRPO); 6 is an outlier that only FlowGRPO's `*_flux_fast` configs adopt, and the author's rationale is frankly empirical: "This is just a hyperparameter I think works well—it's fast and doesn't affect evaluation performance" ([flow_grpo issue #163](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/163)).

**Measured, on group size.** Covered above: G=8 is inside Flow-GRPO's documented G=12/G=6 collapse band, and small groups "produce inaccurate advantage estimates, increasing variance and leading to training collapse" ([arXiv:2505.05470v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05470v5)). Note that DanceGRPO and MixGRPO both run FLUX at G=12, also inside that band — which is itself a candidate explanation for the published FLUX instability.

**Conjecture, and the notes label it as such.** The attractive story — that FLUX.1-dev's guidance distillation lowers per-prompt output entropy, shrinking within-group reward spread, so GRPO's `(r − mean)/std` becomes a ratio of two small noisy quantities dominated by whichever samples happen to deviate — is **not published anywhere**. No source asserts it for guidance-distilled image models; the advantage-collapse literature it borrows from is LLM-side with binary rewards where the failure is literal zero ([Advantage Collapse in GRPO, arXiv:2605.21125](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21125v1)). The supporting pieces exist separately — CFG-distilled students "suffer from a decrease in diversity compared to [their] CFG-free base model" initialization, though measured only by human preference rather than a quantified diversity metric ([arXiv:2410.06084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06084)), and FLUX.1-dev is observed to produce "nearly redundant outputs" capturing "only a narrow subset of the potential distribution" ([arXiv:2606.27371](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27371)) — but the conjunction is unsupported, and there is a counterargument: Flow-GRPO's exploration comes from the injected SDE noise σ_t, which is independent of the policy's own entropy. Equally unsupported are the claims that guidance distillation *specifically* (rather than scale or architecture) causes the fragility, and that 6 steps is specifically where FLUX breaks. A circulating claim that "Flow-GRPO fails on FLUX.2 because likelihood estimation becomes inaccurate when the CFG guidance scale is embedded in the DiT" **could not be located in any primary source** and should not be used.

One correction worth carrying forward, since it bears on the window: the folk belief that *late* steps control rendered glyphs appears to be wrong. The one targeted source says **mid steps** are "most informative for writing placement," while early steps are "coarse and reflect global planning" and late steps "become diffuse due to global refinement" ([FreeText, arXiv:2601.00535](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00535), via search summary). A window pinned to the top of the noise schedule may therefore exclude every step that can move an OCR reward — which is a prediction, not a finding.

## Removing the window fixed one cliff edge of five, and our experiment could not have detected it anyway

The degeneracy is real. With `sde_window_range = (0, num_steps//2) = (0,3)` and `sde_window_size = 3`, the window start is `random.randint(0, 3-3) = random.randint(0,0) ≡ 0`, so the FLUX fast recipe trains a **fixed** window of steps {0,1,2} every rollout, with zero randomisation of position — unlike `geneval_sd3_fast_nocfg` (num_steps=10, range (0,5)) which genuinely samples start ∈ {0,1,2} ([config/grpo.py](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/blob/main/config/grpo.py); [flux_pipeline_with_logprob_fast.py](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/blob/main/flow_grpo/diffusers_patch/flux_pipeline_with_logprob_fast.py)). Nothing in the repo, issues or paper acknowledges this. Our null result does not make the degeneracy a non-finding; it makes it a non-explanation. Four reasons reconcile the two:

**First, the window is load-bearing for a reason that removing it reintroduces.** The high-noise-only restriction exists to dodge two distinct failure modes at once: bf16 log-prob error, which is worse at low noise, argues for excluding late steps from the loss; and residual SDE noise in the image handed to the reward model argues for running late steps as pure ODE. The in-code comment is explicit that the final step "is close to the image, the Gaussian distribution is very sharp, the probability is very large, and precision overflow happens easily, so it does not take part in training" ([flux_pipeline_with_logprob_fast.py](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/blob/main/flow_grpo/diffusers_patch/flux_pipeline_with_logprob_fast.py)). And the window was adopted on FLUX precisely *because* every-step SDE broke: in issue #141 a user reported FLUX rewards failing to rise with bright-spot artifacts, the fix was high-noise-only training, and the reporter confirmed "I tried training only on the high-noise portion, like flow-grpo-fast, and now the reward rises normally" ([flow_grpo issue #141](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/141)). **Our no-window arm trains 5 of 6 steps — i.e. it reconstructs the exact configuration that thread documents as failing on FLUX.** Landing at 0.6169, statistically indistinguishable from the pinned window, is the predicted outcome: it trades a pinned-window pathology for a low-σ-precision pathology.

**Second, the roaming arm is the one that moved, in the direction theory predicts, and we lacked the power to confirm it.** Roaming's +0.0344 over the original is consistent with MixGRPO's published sliding window — "early in training, the window focuses on initial timesteps to allow exploration of global layout and composition, then in mid-to-late training the window slides toward later steps for optimizing fine-grained details" ([MixGRPO, arXiv:2507.21802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21802)) — and with the glyph evidence pointing at mid steps. But at a pooled σ ≈ 0.048 with n=4 and n=3, the standard error of that difference is **0.0367**, so our minimum detectable effect at 80% power is roughly **0.10 OCR points** — a third of Flow-GRPO's entire published SD3.5 OCR gain, and larger than the between-seed spread we are trying to explain. Detecting an effect the size of the roaming point estimate would have required about **31 seeds per arm**. The experiment was underpowered by construction, which is a statement about our design, not about the window.

**Third, five cliff edges, one fix.** Issue #204 alone required beta reduced 100× to 4e-4, `train.cfg = False`, and BF16→FP32 before FLUX training worked; Flow-GRPO documents the G=12 collapse; DanceGRPO documents that noise level "larger than 0.3 sometimes leads to noisy images after RLHF training" ([arXiv:2505.07818v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07818v1)); the FLUX KL path was outright broken until commit `c86e18e` ("fix kl in flux and flux kontext"), which is why **every shipped FLUX config sets `beta = 0`** ([flow_grpo issue #113](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/113), [issue #110](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/110)). When a configuration sits near several cliff edges at once, removing one should not move the mean — and that is precisely the regime in which seed variance becomes bimodal.

**Fourth, the timing of over-optimization makes the terminal metric a coin flip.** Issue #204's quality collapse begins around step 240 of a ~300-step protocol, with reward and eval curves still rising ([flow_grpo issue #204](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/204)). If run-to-run jitter shifts *when* the onset occurs, a fixed terminal checkpoint flips a run from improvement to degradation without any difference in the underlying method — which is a direct mechanism for our 0.5575-vs-0.6507 split, and one that no window change touches.

## Our three- and four-seed arms already exceed every published diffusion-RL seed protocol

Here the comparison runs in our favour, and it should be stated that way. Across ten canonical diffusion/flow RL and reward-finetuning papers — DDPO, DPOK, DRaFT, ReFL, Diffusion-DPO, Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO, SRPO, MixGRPO, DDRL — **not one states a training-seed count, and the string "error bar" appears zero times in all ten full texts.** Every uncertainty figure any of them reports is computed over evaluation samples or human raters, never over independent training runs ([DDPO, arXiv:2305.13301](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13301); [DPOK, arXiv:2305.16381](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16381); [MixGRPO, arXiv:2507.21802](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21802); [DDRL, arXiv:2512.04332](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04332)). In this literature "seed" almost always means *the initial latent noise within a rollout group*, not an independent training run — DanceGRPO uses shared initial noise as an anti-reward-hacking regularizer ([dancegrpo.github.io](https://dancegrpo.github.io/)), TempFlow-GRPO builds group size 24 as "4 seeds × 6 branches" ([arXiv:2508.04324v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04324v2)), and Flow-GRPO hard-codes `config.seed = 42`, never overridden by any SD3 or FLUX config ([config/base.py](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/blob/main/config/base.py)). The field has a methodological *incentive* to suppress exactly the stochasticity multi-seed reporting would measure. **The only multi-seed result in the entire space runs 3 seeds on a toy backbone (Tiny-SD3) while explicitly stating its FLUX campaign is single-seed** ([λ-Controlled GRPO, arXiv:2609.22041v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22041v2)). There is no published between-training-seed standard deviation for any T2I diffusion-RL method on any benchmark — not one number.

Classic deep RL settled this a decade ago at a far higher bar. Henderson et al. ran **5 trials per configuration** with mean ± standard error and showed that two disjoint sets of 5 seeds of the *same* algorithm and hyperparameters can produce statistically distinguishable distributions, while declining to prescribe a fixed number: "While there can be no specific number of trials specified as a recommendation, it is possible that power analysis methods can be used" ([arXiv:1709.06560](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.06560)). Agarwal et al. report that "3-10 runs are prevalent in deep RL," that defensible median comparisons on Atari 100k need "closer to 50–100 runs," that "percentile CIs provide good interval estimates for as few as N = 10 runs," and that fixed seeds are not an escape hatch because they "might benefit certain algorithms more than others" ([arXiv:2108.13264v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.13264v4)). **Two seeds is below the floor of every recommendation in that literature; our n=3–4 arms with reported SDs are above the ceiling of everything published in diffusion RL.**

Can evaluation noise explain our spread? No. On 1017 prompts with a near-binary OCR score at p ≈ 0.6, the binomial standard error of a single evaluation is about **0.0155** — roughly a quarter of the between-seed σ ≈ 0.066 implied by 0.5575 and 0.6507 (|Δ|/√2). Evaluation sampling cannot plausibly produce a 0.093 gap on an eval of that size; the residual is training-trajectory or checkpoint-selection variance. Note also that a two-sample σ has ~1 degree of freedom, so the honest reading of those two seeds is "σ is plausibly 0.03–0.15," not 0.066. And for scale: **0.093 is ≈28% of Flow-GRPO's entire headline SD3.5 OCR improvement** of 0.33 — large enough to flip most of the ablation-table deltas in this literature, if those deltas are also single-seed. Which they are.

The reproduction record corroborates that the published numbers are not tight point estimates. A still-open issue reports the official SD3 OCR config producing a reward "pretty much stuck in place, both during training and eval" on 8×H200 ([flow_grpo issue #227](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/227)); another reports PickScore failing to rise at matched global batch size until DeepSpeed was removed, with the warning "I highly discourage using Deepspeed, as it seems to destabilize training" ([issue #172](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/172)); an attempt to reproduce GenEval 0.95 on 4 GPUs instead of 32, matching every documented hyperparameter and holding throughput constant, plateaus at ~0.90 with visible reward hacking and remains unanswered by maintainers ([issue #230](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/230)). The author's own standard of reproduction is relative rather than absolute: "the most important aspect is the relative change of the model after GRPO compared to before" ([issue #39](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/39)).

## What to change for a trustworthy FLUX number, and what to tell Haotian Ye

**Priority 1 — fix the advantage estimator before touching anything else.** Raise G from 8 toward 24 and prompts/epoch from 4 toward 48. These are the two deviations with direct published failure evidence (Flow-GRPO's G=12/6 collapse; the author's explicit warning that lowering batch or group size can stop the reward rising), and together they are also the 3.75× trajectory-budget gap between our FLUX and SD3.5 arms. If compute forces a choice, raise G first: group size enters the advantage denominator directly. Expect this alone to shrink the spread more than any sampler change.

**Priority 2 — move the surrogate and backward pass to FP32.** Keep bf16 rollout, but compute log-probs for both π_θold and π_θ, and the backward pass, in FP32. This is V-GRPO's production practice and GRPO-Guard's author's specific fix for FLUX over-optimization. It is cheap, it is the one intervention that directly attacks a *run-dependent* noise source, and bf16 rounding is the most parsimonious candidate mechanism for apparent seed variance on FLUX.

**Priority 3 — raise training steps from 6 to at least 10, ideally toward 25.** 6 sits below Flow-GRPO's own turning point and well below every published FLUX recipe; V-GRPO needed extra stabilization at 16. This also narrows the 6-train/28-eval mismatch, which Flow-GRPO's FLUX path makes worse by evaluating at pure ODE (`noise_level = 0`) while training with SDE.

**Priority 4 — adopt a variance-reduction layer rather than a KL anchor.** For FLUX, "the KL penalty alone is insufficient to stabilize training" and our own FlowGRPO-0.04 arm confirms it (spread 0.084, barely different from 0.093). The measured alternatives are V-GRPO's group-shared timestep-noise pairs plus stratified timestep sampling, which cut the coefficient of variation of surrogate magnitude 0.230 → 0.128 and mean within-group CV 0.170 → 0.038; GRPO-Guard's normalized ratio with gradient reweighting; or the Flow-GRPO author's own recommendation of velocity-MSE as the KL term plus EMA — "with these two techniques in place, I barely observe any reward hacking at all," verified "on more generalized prompts and larger-scale models" ([flow_grpo issue #204](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/issues/204)).

**Priority 5 — let the window roam, but do not expect it to rescue the arm.** Our data already says it will not, and the mechanism says why. If it roams, roam it over the full schedule on a MixGRPO-style schedule rather than pinning it high, since the glyph evidence points at mid steps. Treat this as hygiene, not as the fix.

**Priority 6 — change the measurement protocol, not just the training.** Five seeds minimum per FLUX arm, reported as an interval rather than a point; pre-register the checkpoint-selection rule, because over-optimization onset near step 240 of 300 makes a fixed terminal checkpoint a coin flip; log per-step importance-ratio histograms and within-group reward std as live diagnostics; and report held-out OOD quality metrics beside OCR. That last point is not optional given DDRL's Table 4, where both baselines' *higher* OCR on SD3.5-M is explicitly marked as hacking — DanceGRPO at OCR 0.846 with ImageReward 0.80 and Δ-Vote −53.0, FlowGRPO at 0.845 with ImageReward 1.03, against a base model at OCR 0.566 and ImageReward 1.13 ([arXiv:2512.04332v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04332v2)). **A terminal OCR of 0.6507 above a 0.592 baseline is, on that paper's own logic, as likely to be a hacking signature as a win, and we cannot currently tell.**

**What to tell Haotian Ye about the FLUX cell.** Be direct: the FLUX cell is outside DDRL's validated surface in every dimension, and we should report it as an interval with an explicit caveat rather than as a comparable number. Concretely, (i) **DDRL contains no FLUX experiment** — zero occurrences of the string across all three arXiv versions — so our FLUX arms are an extrapolation we undertook, not a reproduction of anything; (ii) DDRL's only image evidence is one SD3.5-M / 512×512 / OCR run per method, with checkpoints "cho[sen] with similar final OCR accuracy for evaluation," making Table 4 an OCR-matched quality comparison rather than an equal-compute one; (iii) the anchor weight is a live question for us, because the paper states the image diffusion-loss weight as 0.001 flatly with no sweep and no justification, 10× away from the video β = 0.01, and the only β sweep reported (β ∈ [0.001, 0.1]) was run for *FlowGRPO's* reverse KL and then inherited — "For DDRL, we simply use the same β as well." There is therefore no published basis for expecting a DDRL anchor strength tuned on SD3.5-M to transfer to FLUX.1-dev, and our FLUX anchor arms (spread 0.084) are consistent with it not transferring.

Two asks worth making of him. First, the §B.3 omissions that block a faithful FLUX port: **which image denoising steps receive gradient** (the "every other step, |𝒯|=10 of T=20" rule is stated for video only, and the image rollout is a 10-step SDE sampler with no |𝒯| reported), and **whether the image rollouts used CFG 4.5** — §3.3 says DDRL does not use CFG, while §B.3 quotes 4.5 only for generating the synthetic anchor data. Both matter doubly on FLUX, which is guidance-distilled and has no unconditional branch. Second, since DDRL reports single runs throughout with no seeds, error bars or confidence intervals, and since our SD3.5 replication lands at a credible +0.188 with a 0.008 spread under the anchor, our most useful contribution to his grid may be the **first between-seed intervals anyone has published for this method family** — offered as a measurement contribution rather than as a FLUX result.

## Conclusion

The sharpest thing this synthesis changes is the question. We came in asking whether our FLUX arms were broken; the answer is that the FLUX+OCR cell was never a measurable cell to begin with. Nobody has published a working FLUX.1-dev + OCR RL result; the one attempt reported it as unresolved on a single seed, and the one independent reproduction reported quality collapse while reward and eval curves rose. Our 0.093 spread is not evidence that we did something wrong — it is the first quantification of something the field has systematically declined to measure, produced by a protocol more rigorous than any of the ten canonical papers in this space. The asymmetry that matters most is not FLUX-high-variance versus SD3.5-low-variance in the abstract; it is that an anchor which collapses SD3.5's spread to 0.008 leaves FLUX's at 0.084, which is a backbone-specific pathology the KL-insufficiency finding on FLUX.1-dev already predicts.

What follows is a reframing of the deliverable rather than a longer debugging list. The FLUX grid cell should be reported as an interval with a stated power limit, accompanied by the observation that its spread exceeds the published effect sizes it would be compared against — and the next FLUX experiment should be designed to reduce variance (G, prompts/epoch, FP32 surrogate, group-shared noise) before it is designed to raise reward. Running more arms at n=3 on a 6-step, G=8 configuration will keep producing differences of 0.03 that we cannot resolve from zero, at roughly 31 seeds per arm of required budget. Equally: because every number in Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO, MixGRPO, BranchGRPO, SRPO and DDRL is a single run with no interval, the right epistemic stance toward the whole comparative table — theirs and ours — is that its smaller deltas have never been shown to exceed run-to-run noise. We are, uncomfortably, better positioned to say that than anyone who has published in this area.
